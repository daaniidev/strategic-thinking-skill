# Strategic thinking fundamentals

This file covers the underlying framework: what a strategy must contain, how
to distinguish the strategic from the tactical, the 6 Ps of strategic
thinking, what it means to set strategic direction, what a strategic mindset
is built on, and how leadership and management relate to each other. It is
the framework that is almost always implicit behind any analysis — it does
not replace SWOT/PESTEL (`references/swot-pestel.md`), Porter/BMC
(`references/porter-canvas.md`), McKinsey 7S (`references/mckinsey-7s.md`),
SMART goals (`references/smart-goals.md`), or the decision-making models
(`references/decision-making.md`), which are covered in their own files.

## What a strategy must contain

Every well-formed strategy covers these five elements; if the report is
missing any of them, it is incomplete:

- **Vision and mission** — where the organization is heading in the long
  term and why it exists; the anchor for everything else.
- **Goals and objectives** — what concrete results are being pursued and
  within what timeframe (see `references/smart-goals.md` for how to
  formulate them).
- **Analysis and assessment** — an honest diagnosis of the internal and
  external situation, typically via SWOT (see `references/swot-pestel.md`).
- **Decision-making and prioritization** — choosing between options and
  ordering what gets tackled first (see `references/decision-making.md`).
- **Communication, implementation and execution** — putting the plan into
  practice and getting the organization to understand and adopt it (see
  `references/strategic-narrative.md` for the communication piece).

## Strategic vs. tactical

The skill must always explicitly label each response as **strategic** or
**tactical** — never blend the two silently. If a request has components
from both time horizons, that is stated openly and each is treated as a
separate block within the same report.

| Strategic (long-term) | Tactical (short-term) |
|---|---|
| Expansion into new markets | A one-off marketing campaign |
| Major investment decision (CAPEX, acquisition) | Employee training |
| Business model redesign | Price adjustment for a promotion |
| Strategic alliance or merger | Optimizing a specific operational process |
| Organization-wide digital transformation | One-off hiring to fill a vacancy |

Rule of thumb: if the decision commits resources in a way that is hard to
reverse and its time horizon exceeds a year, it is strategic; if it is
reversible, narrow in scope and executable within weeks or months, it is
tactical.

## The 6 Ps of strategic thinking

The 6 Ps are a checklist for diagnosing whether a business situation is
being thought through with enough strategic depth. They work both for
analyzing a user request and for structuring the context questions of the
hybrid flow (section 1 of `SKILL.md`).

| P | What it means | Guiding question |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | The underlying reason behind the action, beyond the immediate outcome | Why does this matter to the organization's mission? |
| Position | Where you stand relative to competitors and the market | What advantage or disadvantage do we have against those competing for the same thing? |
| Plan | The concrete actions, timelines and resources required | What will be done, with what resources, and by when? |
| Perspective | A holistic view connecting the internal with the external | How does this fit with the rest of the organization and with the macro environment? |
| Pace | The speed and agility with which implementation can and should happen | How fast do we need to move, and how rigid is execution if the context changes? |
| Performance | How success is measured | What KPIs show whether this is working? |

## Strategic direction

Setting strategic direction serves four functions, regardless of which
analytical framework sits on top of it:

- **Guides decision-making** — provides a shared criterion for choosing
  between options as they arise.
- **Sets goals and objectives** — translates the vision into concrete
  targets to work toward.
- **Shapes organizational culture** — strategic priorities end up molding
  how people behave day to day.
- **Builds trust and credibility** — clear, sustained direction builds
  internal confidence (the team) and external confidence (investors,
  customers, partners).

## The foundation of a strategic mindset

A strategic mindset is not born out of analysis — it rests on four
underlying pillars:

- **Values and beliefs** — what the organization considers non-negotiable
  guides which options are not even considered.
- **Leadership and communication** — without leadership that articulates
  and sustains direction, analysis stays on paper.
- **Employee commitment and satisfaction** — a strategy is only executed
  well if the people implementing it are committed to it.
- **Innovation and adaptability** — the ability to adjust course without
  losing sight of the underlying purpose.

Per section 5 of `SKILL.md`, this foundation can color the general tone of
responses when the user is in a strategic-reflection mode — without forcing
that tone outside of that context.

## Leadership vs. management

These are two complementary functions, not a hierarchy or synonyms. A
strategy needs both to succeed: without leadership there is no direction to
pursue with conviction; without management, direction never translates into
results.

| Leadership | Management |
|---|---|
| Inspires and motivates toward shared goals | Plans, organizes and controls resources and processes |
| Provides vision, inspiration and direction | Provides effective execution |
| Answers "where to, and why" | Answers "how, and with what" |
| Measured in commitment and alignment | Measured in compliance and efficiency |
