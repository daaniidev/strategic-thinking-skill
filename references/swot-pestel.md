# SWOT and PESTEL

## SWOT

SWOT (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats) is a 2x2 matrix that
crosses two axes: the factor's origin (internal / external) and its effect
(positive / negative).

|  | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** (the organization) | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| **External** (the environment) | Opportunities | Threats |

- **Strengths** and **Weaknesses** are internal: they depend on the
  organization and, in principle, are controllable or changeable by it
  (resources, capabilities, processes, talent, current positioning).
- **Opportunities** and **Threats** are external: they come from the
  environment (market, competition, regulation, technology, society), and
  the organization doesn't control them directly — it can only anticipate
  and react to them.

**What it's for:** risk management and decision-making. It gives a quick
snapshot of where the organization stands before deciding where to move
next — which of its own capabilities to leverage, which gaps to fix, which
market windows to seize, and which external risks to watch.

**When to use it as the default starting point:** when the user's
situation is ambiguous or it's an initial diagnosis and it's not yet clear
which more specific framework applies (competition, business model,
internal organization...). SWOT is cheap to build, covers internal and
external at once, and tends to reveal which framework to apply next
(Porter if the threats are competitive, PESTEL if they're macro, 7S if the
weaknesses are organizational).

### Guiding questions by quadrant

Use these to help a real user fill in each quadrant when they don't
already have clear answers.

**Strengths** (internal, positive)
- What do you do better than the competition, or better than a year ago?
- What resources of your own (team, technology, brand, cash, relationships)
  are hard to replicate?
- What do customers tell you they value most about you?

**Weaknesses** (internal, negative)
- What holds you back or makes you lose opportunities over and over?
- Where do you depend on a single person, supplier, or fragile process?
- What would you want to hide from an investor or auditor if you could?

**Opportunities** (external, positive)
- What's changing in the market, technology, or regulation that you could
  seize before others do?
- Is there an underserved customer segment, geography, or channel?
- What trend (PESTEL) is currently working in your favor?

**Threats** (external, negative)
- What move by a competitor, supplier, or regulator would put you in
  trouble?
- What trend (PESTEL) works against you or raises your operating costs?
- What would happen if your main revenue source or key customer
  disappeared?

## PESTEL

PESTEL (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal)
diagnoses the macro environment across six dimensions. Unlike SWOT, it
doesn't look inside the organization: it looks at the external forces that
affect any player in the industry equally.

| Dimension | What it looks at | Example factor |
|---|---|---|
| **Political** | Government stability, fiscal/trade policy, international relations | Import tariffs on a key component |
| **Economic** | Economic cycle, inflation, interest rates, purchasing power | A rate hike that raises the cost of financing expansion |
| **Social** | Demographics, consumption habits, cultural values | An aging population that reduces demand for a youth-oriented product |
| **Technological** | Pace of innovation, automation, digital disruption | The emergence of a technology (e.g., generative AI) that automates a core business task |
| **Environmental (CSR)** | Environmental regulation, sustainability, social responsibility expectations | New emissions regulations that force a redesign of the supply chain |
| **Legal** | Labor law, data protection, intellectual property, industry compliance | A new data protection law taking effect that requires redesigning how customer information is handled |

**What it's for:** strategic planning, risk management, and designing
business actions with a long time horizon. It's the right framework when
the scope of the analysis is explicitly the macro environment (not the
organization or a specific competitor), typically ahead of expansion,
investment, or new market entry decisions.

## SWOT + PESTEL together

These aren't independent frameworks: in practice, PESTEL feeds the
external half of SWOT. The macro environment PESTEL diagnoses across its
six dimensions translates directly into factors in SWOT's external
column — Opportunities and Threats.

Recommended flow when the user's scope includes both internal and
external:

1. Run PESTEL first (or in parallel) to map the macro environment across
   the six dimensions.
2. For each relevant PESTEL factor, ask whether it works for or against
   the specific organization being analyzed.
3. Carry the favorable factors over as Opportunities and the unfavorable
   ones as Threats into the SWOT matrix.
4. Complete Strengths and Weaknesses with the internal diagnosis
   (resources, capabilities, processes), which PESTEL doesn't cover.

This way the final SWOT is anchored in an explicit environmental analysis
instead of loose intuitions about "opportunities" and "threats," and it
keeps both frameworks from being treated as alternatives rather than
complementary.
